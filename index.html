Know the plant

Every cannabis product starts with one plant: Cannabis sativa. There are hundreds of strains, but the part that matters most is the flower of the female plant and the sticky resin on it.

What's inside

The plant makes hundreds of compounds. The ones that act on your brain are cannabinoids, and the two that matter most are THC and CBD. They're fat-soluble, so they hang around: after one use THC can show up in tests for about a week, and with heavy regular use for a month or more.

One plant, a lot of forms

Flower, hash, oils, edibles: they all come from the same plant, just processed differently. Lab-made synthetic cannabinoids are a different thing entirely. In California, adults 21 and over can buy from licensed, state-tested retailers. In plenty of other places it's still illegal.

Your brain on it

Your brain keeps developing until about 25, and it's most sensitive to cannabis while it's still growing. Starting young and using heavily raises the risk of anxiety, depression and dependence. Knowing that is part of the culture too.

Is everyone really doing it?

It can feel like everyone smokes, but the numbers say otherwise. In the US Monitoring the Future survey (2024), about 26% of high school seniors had used cannabis in the past year, the lowest level in three decades, and 7% of 8th graders had. Among adults, about 23% used it in the past year (NSDUH 2022–2024), rising to 37% of 18 to 25 year olds. Most people who use it never develop a problem, but if you start young, the odds go up.

Know the law where you are

In California, adults 21 and over can legally have up to 28.5 grams (about an ounce) of flower and up to 8 grams of concentrate, and grow up to six plants per household. Buy only from licensed retailers and delivery services. More than that, or selling without a license, is still a crime.

Legal doesn't mean anywhere, anytime: no smoking in public or where tobacco is banned, no driving high, and no taking it across state lines, even between two legal states, because cannabis is still illegal under federal law. Every state sets its own rules, so check before you travel.

Filmer

Bud

The bud is the flower of the female plant. It's covered in tiny resin glands called trichomes, which hold the most THC, the compound behind the high. Dried buds are what people call weed; the resin is pressed into hash.

Leaf

The fan leaf is the icon (it's on our logo for a reason), but on its own it carries very little THC.

Stem

The stem holds the leaves and flowers up. It has almost no THC.

Hash

Hash is the plant's resin, collected and pressed into a brown block. It's usually stronger than flower.

THC oil

A concentrated oil extracted with solvents. Very high in THC and low in CBD. It's vaped, smoked or mixed into food, and a little goes a long way.

Edibles

Brownies, gummies, drinks. Eaten cannabis takes 1 to 3 hours to kick in and lasts much longer than smoking. Most bad experiences come from taking more before the first dose has landed, so start low and go slow.

Flower

Flower is the dried, cured bud. You'll see it sorted as indica, sativa or hybrid, though the real effect comes down to each strain's THC and terpenes. Metro Bloomin flower: Blowin' Biscotti with Connected (indica-dominant South Florida OG × Gelato #25, in 3.5 g and 10 g jars and 1 g prerolls), Gushers and Candy Paint.

CBD oil

CBD oil is mostly CBD with little or no THC, so it shouldn't get you high, but labels aren't always accurate. Its legal status varies by country.

Synthetic cannabinoids

Spice and K2 are lab-made chemicals sprayed on plant matter. They're not cannabis, they're far stronger and unpredictable, and they've caused seizures, heart attacks and psychosis. Not the same thing, and not worth it.

Short term

Everyone reacts differently. Some people feel relaxed, giggly or hungry; others get anxious, paranoid, nauseous or a racing heart. It usually passes as it wears off.

It slows your reactions and focus, so never drive high. Mixing it with alcohol or other drugs makes it stronger and less predictable.

Long term

Heavy use over a long time can affect memory, focus and motivation.

In a young, still-developing brain some of those effects can last. Long-term heavy use is also linked to anxiety, depression and, for people who are vulnerable to it, psychosis.

Dependence

Use a lot and your tolerance climbs, so you need more to feel the same. Starting young and using daily makes dependence more likely.

Quitting after heavy use can mean a few rough weeks: bad sleep, irritability, anxiety, poor focus. If it starts crowding out the rest of your life, that's the sign to step back or talk to someone.

THC

THC (tetrahydrocannabinol) is what gets you high. It changes mood, perception, memory and reaction time while it's active. The higher the THC, the higher the chance of a bad time: anxiety, paranoia and, in rare cases, psychosis.

CBD

CBD (cannabidiol) doesn't get you high, and it may soften some of THC's edge, like anxiety. Research is still catching up on what it can and can't do.
